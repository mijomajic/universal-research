# Academic research mode

Literature-oriented research across disciplines. Related to science mode when the literature is empirical, but this lens also covers humanities, theory, methods debates, and "who argued what."

## What this mode is for

Discovery, seminal vs recent work, citation chaining, review papers, primary sources, schools of thought, methodological differences, unresolved debates, bibliographies. Prefer full text when the claim depends on argument or numbers, not abstracts.

## Method

1. **Name the conversation** — field, period, and the question as scholars would recognize it.
2. **Find orientation** — handbook chapters, annual reviews, meta-reviews, syllabi, Stanford Encyclopedia-style entries when they exist.
3. **Seed with 3–8 core works** — mix seminal and recent. Note why each is core.
4. **Chain** — who they cite, who cites them (as tools allow). Record the chain so claims are not orphaned.
5. **Map schools** — competing definitions and methods, not just competing results.
6. **Debates** — what is actually unresolved vs what is textbook.
7. **Bibliography** — consistent citation; mark items you did not read in full.

## vs science mode

Use **science** when the user wants the empirical answer (does creatine change cognition?). Use **academic** when they want the literature as an object (how has remote-work productivity been studied?). Many requests need both: academic to find the corpus, science to weigh designs.

## Sources

Publisher pages, repositories, library records, Google Scholar-class indexes, books, critical editions. Firecrawl helps with open HTML/PDF URLs. Do not pretend a scraped abstract is a reading of the article.

## Pitfalls

- Recency bias that drops the paper everyone still argues with.
- Citation-count worship.
- One review article treated as the field.
