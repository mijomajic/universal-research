# Market research mode

Customer and category research without a mandatory business framework. ICP, VOC, JTBD, personas, TAM/SAM/SOM, and positioning maps are **optional** when they help; they are not default sections.

## What this mode is for

Category definition, structure, segments, problems and desired outcomes, buying behavior, triggers, objections, pricing and demand signals, substitutes, maturity, positioning, distribution, gaps, and the **language customers use**.

## Method

1. **Category** — what buyers compare this to; adjacent substitutes.
2. **Who** — segments only as coarse as the evidence supports. Do not invent a persona novel.
3. **Jobs / problems / outcomes** — what they are trying to get done; failure modes.
4. **Behavior** — triggers, objections, switching, workarounds, frequency. Qualitative first unless you have real quantitative data.
5. **Value and price** — list prices, discounting talk, "too expensive / not worth it" language. Do not treat a blog TAM as a fact.
6. **Structure** — incumbents, channels, regulation if it binds, maturity (fad vs category).
7. **Gaps** — underserved jobs or segments **backed by evidence**, not a brainstorm slide.

## Sources

Mix: first-party (pricing, onboarding, help center), reviews, support/community, Reddit/forums for language, interviews/transcripts if available, app-store text, specialist trade press. Use X for launches and live complaint bursts, not for prevalence.

Social and reviews are **qualitative**. Repeating phrases across independent threads are stronger than one viral post.

## Do not

- Run a science workflow on a product-pain question.
- Output an ICP canvas because this file exists.
- Confuse "people on Reddit hate it" with "the market is dying."
