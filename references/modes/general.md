# General research mode

Fallback lens when the question is not strongly scientific, market, technical, visual, etc. Also a base layer under other modes.

## What this mode is for

- What is known about X?
- History, current state, major arguments, unresolved questions
- Who the entities are and how they relate
- Competing explanations without a single "correct" framework

## Method

1. **Scope** — time window, geography, and what "X" includes/excludes.
2. **Landscape** — entities, concepts, dates, factions. A short concept map beats a long narrative dump.
3. **Chronology** — only if change-over-time is part of the question.
4. **Source discovery** — mix classes: reference works, specialist writing, primary documents, data, informed commentary. Avoid a pure SEO-roundup diet.
5. **Competing explanations** — steelman at least two if the topic is contested.
6. **Uncertainty** — distinguish settled description from live debate from unknown.
7. **Gaps** — what a serious next pass would need.

## Output instincts

Lead with the user's question. A landscape + arguments + open questions is enough unless they asked for a specific artifact. Do not bolt on TAM, PRISMA, or competitor matrices.

## Pitfalls

- Treating the first encyclopedia-style page as complete.
- Collapsing a debate into a fake consensus.
- Over-weighting recency on historical questions (or under-weighting it on fast-moving ones).
