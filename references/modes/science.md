# Science research mode

For empirical and scientific claims. Calibrate conclusions to study quality. Overlaps academic mode but stays on **evidence about the world**, not the history of a literature as such.

## What this mode is for

Hypotheses, study design, randomized vs observational evidence, controls, confounding, power, effect size, practical significance, replication, meta-analysis, systematic reviews, publication bias, preprints, mechanisms, human vs animal/in-vitro, consensus vs uncertainty.

## Method

1. **Pin the question** — population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, setting (PICO-like only when it fits).
2. **Find the highest-leverage evidence first** — recent systematic reviews/meta-analyses, then key RCTs, then other human data. Mechanistic and animal evidence explain, they rarely settle human claims.
3. **Read past the abstract** when magnitude, inclusion criteria, or limitations matter.
4. **Appraise** — design, bias, power, conflicts, whether the outcome is the one the user cares about.
5. **Effect size** — direction is not enough; say how large and in whom. Statistical vs practical significance.
6. **Replication and reviews** — one flashy trial is a start. Note pre-registration and publication bias when relevant.
7. **Preprints** — eligible, labeled, extra uncertainty.
8. **Counterevidence** — actively search for nulls and failed replications.

## Sources

Primary papers, Cochrane/PRISMA-like reviews, clinical guidelines, trial registries, specialist societies. Use Firecrawl to pull full pages you can actually open. Do **not** use Reddit, X, or blogs as scientific proof. They can help you find papers or patient-relevant outcome language.

If a paper index (Firecrawl `research` or similar) is **hosted-only**, do not call it against the household API. Find papers another way and scrape what the local kit can reach.

## Output instincts

Lead with the evidence picture and confidence, then limitations. "Insufficient human evidence" is a valid result. Do not give dosing or clinical advice beyond what the user asked and the evidence supports.
